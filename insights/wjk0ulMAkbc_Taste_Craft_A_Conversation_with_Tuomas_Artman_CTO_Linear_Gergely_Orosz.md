# Overview

## Summary
Tuomas Artman, CTO of Linear, and Gergely Orosz discuss building quality software, taste in product development, and AI's impact on engineering.

## Key Takeaway
Quality becomes competitive advantage when AI commoditizes feature velocity; taste cannot be automated.

# Insights

## AI and Quality Paradox
- AI enables shipping every feature request instantly, but saying no becomes more important than ever.
- Quality erodes silently without AB tests; users gradually switch to better experiences when features match.
- 10% of Linear's bugs auto-fix via AI agents, rising toward 100% within years for simple bugs.

## Zero Bug Policy Implementation
- Bugs created at constant rate; fixing immediately versus delayed costs same effort, avoids accumulation.
- Zero bug policy requires stopping feature work temporarily until backlog clears, then maintaining discipline.
- Bugs assigned automatically to responsible engineer; highest priority work that morning, fixed within hours usually.

## Quality Wednesdays Practice
- Every engineer must find and fix one quality issue weekly, cannot use assigned bugs.
- Found 35 quality problems in single small menu; reveals thousands of polish issues lurk.
- Side effect: engineers constantly hunt quality issues while building features, preventing future regressions.

## Measuring Quality Philosophy
- Quality affects revenue only when competition exists; impossible to AB test gradual user erosion.
- Uber's two-pixel correction caught by engineer showed culture of caring before hypergrowth diluted standards.
- High-quality product becomes competitive moat when AI enables anyone to match feature sets quickly.

## Hiring and Culture Building
- One-week paid work trial filters for product thinking; many excellent candidates self-select out, that's ideal.
- All engineers access customer Slack channels; all customer meetings recorded and tagged for searchability.
- Hire specifically for taste and quality mindset; engineers must think like mini-PMs from day one.

## Engineering Role Evolution
- Product engineers replace specialized roles as AI handles QA, DevOps, and code generation tasks.
- Future engineers need customer empathy and product sense more than ability to pipe data between systems.
- Four-year exponential AI growth suggests coding skill becomes commodity; taste and judgment remain scarce.

## Taste as Differentiator
- AI lacks concept of time; cannot feel user frustration from two-second delays or unnatural animations.
- Instant hover highlight plus 150ms fade-out creates quality feel; AI produces correct but tasteless animations.
- Human interface guidelines from Apple provide best foundation for UX quality when designing products.

## Practical Quality Mechanics
- Never ship customer feature requests as-is; group feedback to find root cause, design solution.
- Building for yourself works early; scaling requires customer exposure through recordings and direct access.
- Quality Wednesday, zero bugs, and customer access create system where quality becomes embedded culture.
